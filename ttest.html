Mean 1 is the class writing-hand mean, Yes-group mean, and writing-hand mean, respectively. Mean 2 is the professor’s benchmark, No-group mean, and other-hand mean, respectively. The Yes/No comparison uses Welch’s independent-samples t-test; p-values are two-sided.

Three test write-ups

1. Writing-hand mean vs. benchmark

Direction: The benchmark mean (8.000) is higher than the class writing-hand mean (6.333).

Size: The difference is 1.667 counts.

Belief: The p-value is .001, which is below .05, so the difference is statistically significant.

Interpretation: Our class wrote the phrase fewer times than the benchmark, and the difference is statistically significant.

2. Writing-hand counts: Yes vs. No

Direction: The Yes group mean (6.400) is slightly higher than the No group mean (6.250).

Size: The difference is 0.150 counts.

Belief: The p-value is .848, which is above .05, so the difference is not statistically significant.

Interpretation: The Yes group wrote slightly more than the No group, but the difference is very small and not statistically significant.

3. Writing hand vs. other hand

Direction: The writing-hand mean (6.333) is higher than the other-hand mean (2.556).

Size: The difference is 3.778 counts.

Belief: The p-value is less than .001, which is below .05, so the difference is statistically significant.

Interpretation: Students wrote more with their writing hand than with their other hand, and the difference is statistically significant.

In plain words

Our class wrote the phrase fewer times than Dr. Jacques's benchmark of 8. The Yes and No groups had almost the same results, with no statistically significant difference. Students wrote more with their writing hand than with their other hand, and this difference was statistically significant.

What I would do differently

Our study had only nine students, so the sample size was very small. The Yes and No groups were also small, which could affect the results. Everyone used their writing hand first and their other hand second, so practice or tiredness may have affected the results. We also collected data from only one class, in one room, on one day. If I did this study again, I would include more students, change the order of the hands, and collect data from different classes and settings. This would help make the study more reliable.
